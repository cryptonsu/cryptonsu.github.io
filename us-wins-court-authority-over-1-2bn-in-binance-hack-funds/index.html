US wins court authority over $1.2bn in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department obtained court authority over more than $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, while efforts to trace and freeze the wider theft continue.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 19, 2026 · 06:15 UTC3 min read#21b025

The U.S. Justice Department obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while efforts to trace the funds continue. Its Nov. 12 announcement describes a legal step over part of the stolen assets, not a completed return to victims. The Associated Press report says a judge would determine who has a legitimate claim if the government files a forfeiture action.

From hack to court-authorized restraint

The sequence began on or about Oct. 29, when, according to the Justice Department, North Korean military actors known to the FBI as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The AP reported the theft at about $6.3 billion and said the FBI had attributed it to TraderTraitor. That attribution identifies the group U.S. authorities blame; it does not itself decide ownership of any recovered assets.

After the theft, law enforcement and virtual asset service providers worked to freeze funds. A freeze can block assets from being moved through a participating provider. The Justice Department says combined U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far. Within that total, the department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. The figures describe overlapping stages of the same recovery effort, not separate pools of money.

The court authorization gives the government legal authority to pursue or hold the specified assets. It does not, by itself, establish that all the crypto has been transferred into government custody or returned to Binance customers. The AP reports that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then considers who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. That process matters because the theft and the final ownership decision are distinct questions.

Tracing continues across crypto services

The Justice Department says investigators are still tracing, freezing, seizing and seeking forfeiture of the stolen assets. It says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Bridges move assets between blockchain networks; mixers pool or route transactions to make their paths harder to follow. Exchanges and other virtual asset service providers can help authorities interrupt transfers when they identify associated addresses.

Last week, the FBI, Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation issued an alert urging providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses the group was using to launder the Binance funds. That request makes providers part of the response: investigators trace wallet activity, share identified addresses, and ask services to stop linked transfers. The Justice Department says those efforts are ongoing. Until forfeiture proceedings determine ownership, the court-authorized amount should be understood as assets subject to legal action, not a confirmed payout.

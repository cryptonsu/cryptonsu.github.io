NYSE taps Avalanche for tokenized stock settlement

The NYSE plans to use Avalanche ledger technology to clear and settle trades on a tokenized securities venue, linking multiple asset networks to one settlement system.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 11, 2026 · 10:47 UTC3 min read#4457d4

The New York Stock Exchange has selected Avalanche technology for the clearing and settlement ledger of a planned tokenized securities venue, a move that puts blockchain infrastructure behind the exchange’s push toward round-the-clock trading. In an announcement from Avalanche on Nov. 9, the company said its ledger technology will support the clearing firm behind the NYSE-affiliated platform.

The process has distinct steps. The venue matches buyers and sellers. A clearing firm then manages the trade’s completion, while the ledger records the transaction and settles it. CoinDesk reported the platform will connect stocks issued on multiple blockchains to a custom NYSE blockchain built with Avalanche technology. That makes Avalanche the settlement layer, not the only network where tokenized shares may be issued.

What role will Avalanche play in the trades?

Avalanche will provide the core ledger technology for the clearing infrastructure, according to the company’s announcement. NYSE Head of Digital Issuance Dennis Lee said the ledger needs to be fast and reliable, with predictable costs and control over participation. CoinDesk reported the NYSE plans a custom blockchain rather than settling trades on Avalanche’s public network.

Settlement is the step that completes a trade by recording the exchange of the asset and payment. In this design, the ledger is the shared record used to complete that step. Think of it as a transaction book: the venue matches the order, and the settlement system records that the trade is complete. The platform is being designed for around-the-clock trading and settlement, subject to regulatory approval, The Block reported.

Can securities issued elsewhere use the platform?

Yes. The platform is designed to support equities issued on a range of blockchains, with trades recorded and settled through the NYSE’s Avalanche-based ledger, CoinDesk reported. The issuance network and settlement ledger therefore have separate jobs: one represents the security, while the other records the completed trade.

The NYSE is also working with transfer agents and other firms on issuer-sponsored tokenized securities, The Block reported. Those partners support functions tied to issuing and maintaining securities; the Avalanche ledger handles the trade record and settlement infrastructure. The exchange has said it is choosing different technologies for different parts of the platform.

What still has to happen before trading starts?

The platform remains planned and subject to regulatory approval. The Nov. 9 announcement confirms the technology selection, but does not establish that tokenized stock trading has begun. The ledger choice is one piece of a larger system that must connect order matching, clearing, settlement, custody and the firms responsible for the securities.

For crypto, the development is a defined institutional use of blockchain: Avalanche technology is slated to underpin settlement for a regulated exchange venue, while the venue may accept assets issued on other networks. The arrangement puts the blockchain behind market plumbing rather than replacing the exchange’s trading engine.

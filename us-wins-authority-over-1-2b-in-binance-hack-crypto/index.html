US Wins Authority Over $1.2B in Binance Hack Crypto

The Justice Department says courts have authorized seizure or restraint of $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, part of $2.7 billion frozen so far.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 13, 2026 · 11:52 UTC3 min read#3f8dbd

The US Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as investigators pursue funds from an October hack. The department’s announcement on the Binance seizure says government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets so far.

The distinction matters: court authorization to seize or restrain funds does not mean the entire amount has been returned to Binance or its customers. The Associated Press reported that recovered assets can go through a forfeiture process, in which the government makes a claim and a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to them. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are continuing.

How the seizure process works

First, investigators trace cryptocurrency moving from the wallets involved in the theft. The FBI has urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions linked to addresses used to launder the Binance funds. Those providers can help stop assets moving through exchanges and other services. A freeze restricts movement; it does not by itself settle who owns the funds.

Next, prosecutors seek legal authority over identified assets. In this case, the department says it has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion within the larger pool of more than $2.7 billion frozen by public and private efforts. The remaining assets are still being pursued. Officials say the hackers continue to move funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, which can make tracing and intervention harder.

Finally, forfeiture can determine whether seized property can be transferred to the government and, where appropriate, returned to rightful claimants. The AP’s account of the process says a judge decides who has a legitimate claim. The Justice Department’s announcement does not say that the Binance customers have already been repaid.

What the figures say about the hack

The department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The FBI had previously attributed the theft to the group. The $1.2 billion under court-authorized seizure or restraint is therefore one part of the response, while the more than $2.7 billion frozen figure includes combined government and private-sector action.

For customers, the figures mark progress in limiting the movement of stolen assets, but they do not establish how much will ultimately be recovered or when. The tracing and forfeiture work remains ongoing, and the announcement gives no timetable for a final court decision or repayment.

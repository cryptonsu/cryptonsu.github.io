Swap Slippage: When to Use Auto or Custom

Auto slippage vs custom slippage: use auto for liquid major pairs, and set a custom value for transfer-tax, thin-token or stablecoin swaps needing a tighter floor.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 26, 2026 · 15:36 UTC3 min read#16b75d

For a swap between liquid major tokens, use auto slippage; set a custom slippage tolerance for fee-on-transfer tokens, thin long-tail tokens, or stablecoin pairs where you want a tighter minimum received.

That setting controls how far the swap’s execution price can move from its quote before the trade fails. Say you quote a swap of 1,000 USDC at 0.5% slippage tolerance. The minimum received is 995 USDC. If the swap would deliver less, it reverts instead of completing below that floor.

The quote is the starting output, and the tolerance sets how much less you are willing to accept. Think of the minimum received as a floor under the swap: raising the tolerance lowers the floor, while reducing it raises the floor.

When does auto slippage fit?

Auto slippage suits a typical swap in a liquid, standard pair because the setting can adjust to the conditions of the route. A route is the path used to exchange one token for another across available liquidity. More pool depth generally gives a swap more room to execute without moving the price as much.

Auto cannot know your personal price limit or every token’s special transfer behaviour. A decentralized exchange aggregator such as KyberSwap can find routes across supported liquidity sources and networks, but you still need to choose a tolerance that fits the token and the trade.

On a thin route, consider execution cost alongside price protection: kyberswap fees belong in that comparison, while slippage determines the minimum output you will accept. KyberSwap’s settings are useful to consider when deciding whether the route can clear your chosen floor.

When should you set a custom value?

Custom slippage fits when the token, pool or price floor calls for a more deliberate limit. A stablecoin pair usually needs very low slippage because the tokens are intended to trade near the same value; a looser tolerance can permit more price loss than you intended. A volatile long-tail token may need more room because its pool can be thin and its price can move more during execution.

Fee-on-transfer tokens need special attention. Each transfer deducts a fee from the amount being moved, so the swap may deliver less than a standard quote suggests. The received amount is cut by that transfer tax. Your slippage tolerance must exceed the tax or the output can fall below minimum received and every swap will revert. Set a custom value that accounts for the token’s transfer fee, while keeping the minimum output within the loss you are prepared to accept.

The trade-off is direct: higher tolerance lets a swap fill under a wider range of price changes, but allows a worse price; lower tolerance protects the price floor, but makes the swap more likely to fail. Pool depth, token behaviour and network conditions all affect how much room execution needs. A setting that works for one pair or network may not fit another.

Before confirming, check the quoted output and the minimum received together. For a liquid major pair with standard transfers, auto is a reasonable starting choice. For a taxed token, a thin pool or a stablecoin pair where you want a tight floor, set custom slippage and choose a minimum you are willing to accept. If the quote cannot clear that floor, let the swap revert and reconsider the route or the trade.

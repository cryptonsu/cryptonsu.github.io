US wins authority over $1.2B in Binance hack crypto

The Justice Department says courts authorized seizure or restraint of more than $1.2 billion from a $6.3 billion Binance theft, as freezes and tracing continue.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 16, 2026 · 09:42 UTC3 min read#62ec8e

U.S. authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as investigators and private-sector partners continue tracing the funds. The Justice Department’s announcement says the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and attributes it to North Korean military hackers the FBI calls “TraderTraitor.”

The reported theft is about $6.3 billion, according to the Associated Press. The Justice Department describes it as more than $6 billion. The seizure authority does not mean that all the stolen cryptocurrency has been recovered or returned to Binance or its users. It allows authorities to pursue assets through legal process while efforts to trace and restrain them continue.

How the freeze and seizure authority fit together

The process has several steps. First, investigators trace cryptocurrency linked to the theft as it moves between addresses and services. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors have continued laundering the assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those services can form part of the path investigators follow.

Second, law enforcement works with virtual asset service providers to stop assets moving beyond reach. The department says combined U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen funds. That total is larger than the amount now covered by court authorization: more than $1.2 billion of the frozen assets.

Third, the court-authorized seizure or restraint gives the government a legal route to take control of specified assets or prevent their movement. Think of a freeze as putting a hold on funds while a seizure is the legal step to take them into government custody. The department says the work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the cryptocurrency is still underway.

What the $1.2 billion figure means

The key figure is not the total theft or the total frozen. It is the amount for which the Justice Department says it has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain. The AP reports that the government would typically file a forfeiture claim with a judge, who would decide who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The announcement does not say that those funds have been returned to victims.

The gap between the reported $6.3 billion theft and the more than $2.7 billion frozen also matters. The public figures leave a substantial portion outside the announced freeze total. Authorities say tracing and recovery efforts continue, while the hackers move assets through services including bridges, mixers and exchanges. The figures describe the status reported by officials on Nov. 12, not a final accounting of what Binance or its users will recover.

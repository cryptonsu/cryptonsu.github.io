US wins court authority over $1.2bn in stolen Binance crypto

The Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of assets stolen from Binance, as efforts to trace and recover them continue.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 16, 2026 · 13:22 UTC3 min read#58488a

The U.S. Justice Department has obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, while efforts to trace and recover the assets continue. The department said on Nov. 12 that U.S. agencies and private-sector partners had frozen more than $2.7 billion in total. Its announcement on the Binance asset recovery describes the $1.2 billion as court-authorized seizure or restraint, rather than saying the forfeiture process is complete.

How the recovery process works

First, investigators trace cryptocurrency linked to the theft as it moves between wallets and services. The Justice Department says it worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the funds. These providers include businesses that handle digital assets. They can help identify or freeze assets associated with flagged addresses.

Next, freezing restricts movement of the assets. It does not by itself establish who legally owns them or return them to Binance or its customers. The department says U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion. That total includes the more than $1.2 billion covered by court authority to seize or restrain.

Seizure and forfeiture are legal steps that determine whether the government can take control of assets and who may have a valid claim to them. The Associated Press reported on the FBI’s seizure announcement and noted that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The theft and the amount now held

The Justice Department says members of the North Korean military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around Oct. 29. The FBI had attributed the theft to the group. The $1.2 billion figure is therefore a portion of the reported loss, not the total stolen or the total frozen.

The distinction matters for anyone tracking whether the funds have been recovered. More than $2.7 billion has been frozen through joint U.S. government and private-sector work, while more than $1.2 billion of that amount has court authorization for seizure or restraint. Those figures describe different stages of the process.

The recovery effort is still active. The Justice Department says TraderTraitor actors continue to launder assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, and authorities are continuing to trace, freeze, seize and seek forfeiture of the funds. A bridge moves assets between blockchain networks; a mixer obscures transaction links. Each can complicate tracing, which is why investigators are asking VASPs to block transactions connected to the identified addresses.

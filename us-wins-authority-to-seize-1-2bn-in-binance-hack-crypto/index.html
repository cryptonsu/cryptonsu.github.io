US wins authority to seize $1.2bn in Binance hack crypto

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds, while agencies and crypto firms have frozen more than $2.7 billion.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 14, 2026 · 14:16 UTC3 min read#aba29a

The Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. That legal step can stop funds from moving while the government pursues a claim to them. In its November 12 announcement, the department said tracing and recovery work is continuing as the stolen assets move through crypto services.

The sequence starts with investigators tracing funds linked to the October 29 theft. The FBI attributes the theft to North Korean military hackers it calls TraderTraitor. The Justice Department says the hackers have continued laundering assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those services can move funds between networks, obscure transaction trails or enable trading. US agencies and law enforcement partners have worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to identify and freeze funds connected to the theft.

More than $2.7 billion is frozen

The Justice Department says combined government and private-sector work has frozen more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets. The more than $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is part of that frozen total, not an additional recovery. The department has authority to seize or restrain those funds; it has not said they have been returned to Binance or to affected users.

The distinction matters. A freeze blocks movement, while a seizure or restraint is a legal step toward government control of assets. The Associated Press reports that the US typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. That process means the court authorization does not, by itself, settle who ultimately receives the cryptocurrency.

The rest of the theft remains under investigation

The FBI put the theft at about $6.3 billion. The amount now frozen is substantial, but it is not the full reported loss. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets are ongoing. It also says investigators are working with service providers to block transactions involving addresses used by TraderTraitor to launder the Binance funds.

For crypto holders, the case shows how recovery can depend on more than tracking public blockchain transactions. Investigators need cooperation from services that can intervene when funds reach them, then legal authority to restrain or seize assets. Even after those steps, a forfeiture process may be needed to establish who can claim the recovered funds. The Associated Press account says a judge determines legitimate claims in that process.

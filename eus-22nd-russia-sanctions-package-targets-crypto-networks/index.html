EU’s 22nd Russia sanctions package targets crypto networks

The European Commission proposed restrictions on Russian banks and crypto networks, aiming to curb war financing and close routes used to evade EU sanctions.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 27, 2026 · 18:33 UTC3 min read#bad37f

The European Commission proposed new sanctions on 23 November that would target crypto and payment networks linked to Russia, as part of its 22nd sanctions package. The proposal also extends a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks. In its statement on the 22nd package, Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said the measures would focus on Russia and third countries helping it evade sanctions.

How would the crypto measures work?

The proposal targets crypto and payment networks as part of a broader effort to restrict financial routes that could help Russia evade EU sanctions. Von der Leyen did not name specific platforms or describe a technical process for blocking transactions. She said a Crypto Sanctions Task Force had been launched to support the work.

That leaves key details unresolved in the public announcement: it does not identify which platforms or third-country providers could be affected, or set out how the proposed restrictions would apply to them. The Commission’s stated focus is on networks in Russia and in countries that help Russia evade sanctions.

What would change for Russian banks?

The package would extend the EU transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove all 60 from SWIFT, the international financial messaging system. The transaction ban would restrict dealings with those banks; removal from SWIFT would cut their access to that messaging network. The Commission did not list the banks in von der Leyen’s statement.

The crypto and bank measures sit alongside other financial restrictions in the package. Together, they are designed to make it harder for Russia to access financial services and payment channels, according to the Commission. The announcement does not specify the platforms or payment networks that would be covered.

Are the proposed restrictions already in force?

No. The European Commission has proposed the measures, but they need approval from all 27 EU member states before they can take effect. The Associated Press report published by the Los Angeles Times said the approval process can take weeks and the measures may change before adoption.

What else is in the package?

The proposal includes more than financial restrictions. Von der Leyen said it would list over 70 additional vessels in Russia’s shadow fleet and ban Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at sports events in the EU. It would also restrict Russian sponsors’ financial flows to European sports and cultural associations.

Those measures remain proposals too. For crypto users and providers, the immediate development is the Commission’s plan to target networks it says help Russia evade sanctions; the public announcement does not yet say which services would be affected or when any restrictions would begin.

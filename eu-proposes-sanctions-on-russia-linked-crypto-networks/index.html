EU proposes sanctions on Russia-linked crypto networks

The Commission’s proposed 22nd sanctions package would target crypto and payment networks in Russia and countries helping it evade EU measures.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 00:02 UTC3 min read#c014a8

The European Commission has proposed a new sanctions package that would target crypto and payment networks in Russia and third countries accused of helping Russia evade EU restrictions. President Ursula von der Leyen said the proposal focuses on financial routes used to fund Russia’s war in Ukraine, according to the European Commission’s statement on its 22nd sanctions package, issued on November 23.

How would the proposed crypto measures work?

The Commission says it would target crypto and payment networks in Russia and in third countries that help Russia evade sanctions. In practice, crypto services can provide ways to transfer value between users, while payment networks connect parties moving funds. The proposal seeks to put pressure on the services and networks involved in those routes. The Commission has not specified in its statement which platforms would be covered or set out the detailed rules for applying the restrictions.

That distinction matters: the announcement describes a proposed restriction on networks and services, not a blanket ban on owning or using cryptocurrency. The Commission also says it has launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support the work. It has not provided further detail in the statement about the task force’s membership or powers.

What is the status of the proposal?

The measures are not yet in force. The Associated Press report on the proposal says all 27 EU member countries must endorse it before it takes effect. That process can take weeks, and the package may change during negotiations.

The crypto proposal sits within a wider financial plan. Von der Leyen said the Commission wants to extend a transaction ban to about 60 Russian banks and remove those banks from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use to send payment instructions internationally. SWIFT is like a shared address book and message channel: it helps banks coordinate payments, but does not itself move the money. The announcement does not say the same mechanism would apply to crypto networks.

Why include third-country networks?

The proposal’s reach beyond Russia reflects the Commission’s stated concern that services in other countries can help Russia evade existing sanctions. The announcement does not name a country or a crypto platform in connection with the new measures. It says the EU wants to focus on Russia while also including third countries that assist evasion, and to align enforcement with international partners.

The package also proposes restrictions on Russian teams and athletes competing under the Russian flag at EU sporting events, alongside measures on energy services, shipping and visas. Those measures remain proposals too. For crypto firms and users, the immediate development is a planned expansion of sanctions enforcement across networks and payment services, with the final scope dependent on agreement among EU governments.

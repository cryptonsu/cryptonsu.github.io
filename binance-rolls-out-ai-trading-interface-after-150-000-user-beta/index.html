Binance rolls out AI trading interface after 150,000-user beta

Binance rolled out its free AI interface to global English-language users after about 150,000 beta testers, adding personalized market context, portfolio summaries and alerts.

By Crypto Readout EditorialOct 13, 2026 · 08:58 UTC3 min read#221cb4

Binance rolled out its free AI interface to global English-language users on Oct. 12, after about 150,000 people tested it, adding personalized market information and portfolio tools to the exchange. The Block reported the beta began Sept. 7, citing a Binance spokesperson.

The interface changes what information users see based on the assets they follow and their experience level. It can show portfolio performance, explain market moves, and provide alerts and briefings. Binance’s official announcement describes the system as a customizable set of layouts, widgets and market context. Additional language support is still rolling out, The Block reported.

How does Binance AI tailor the interface?

It starts with the user’s interests and activity, then adjusts the information and tools displayed. Newer users may see simpler explanations and prompts; active traders can see deeper market context, data views and product modules. Users can also rearrange widgets to choose which tools, updates and educational content appear.

For example, a portfolio summary can recap the previous day’s performance alongside events affecting top holdings. A top-movers view shows 24-hour price changes across those holdings, with AI-generated analysis personalized to assets the user holds or follows. Smart tooltips explain features in context, such as when someone visits a product page for the first time.

What can users get from it?

The suite includes voice alerts for tracked assets and on-demand market briefings in text or audio. Binance says briefings refresh every four hours. A feature called Master Trade lets users explore selected investors’ and traders’ profiles to see how they approach asset allocation. The company presents these as ways to bring market information into the Binance interface.

The launch announcement describes information, explanations and interface features. It does not describe Binance AI as placing trades automatically. Binance’s terms say AI outputs may contain errors or outdated information and should not be relied on alone for decisions. The company also says those outputs do not constitute investment advice.

Who can use the full release?

Binance said the rollout was for global English-language users, while support for additional languages was still being introduced. Its announcement says the features are being progressively rolled out to eligible users on the app and website. On the app, users can access them by updating to the latest version; Binance says personalization begins adapting to usage.

The beta’s reported 150,000 testers give a measure of how many people tried the product before the wider release. The feature set centers on helping users navigate market data inside Binance. Users remain responsible for their own investment decisions, the exchange’s terms say.

Tether backs SCRYPT’s expansion into institutional payments

Tether invested in Swiss platform SCRYPT to support its move into third-party payments, extending stablecoin settlement toward fiat accounts for institutions.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 22, 2026 · 01:23 UTC2 min read#4c6829

SCRYPT’s existing payment flow takes in fiat or a supported stablecoin, quotes a currency conversion, screens the transfer and sends the funds to a wallet, custodian or bank. Tether said in its Nov. 19 announcement that it has made a strategic investment in the Swiss firm to support its expansion into third-party payments.

SCRYPT’s service lets payment firms collect money in one currency, move it using stablecoins and receive local currency in their own accounts, according to Crypto.news’ report on the deal. SCRYPT says its “same-day settlement” designation covers crediting funds on its ledger and final settlement of the stablecoin transfer on-chain. Delivery times vary by payment route.

The investment builds on SCRYPT’s existing liquidity, foreign exchange and cross-border settlement services. Its institutional platform combines payments, trading, custody and asset management across fiat currencies, stablecoins and other digital assets. Tether’s announcement says the platform offers 24-hour spot, over-the-counter and foreign exchange trading, plus segregated custody in Switzerland secured using multi-party computation.

The planned expansion adds fiat accounts and payment services for other companies’ customers. Crypto.news reported that a SCRYPT job listing describes virtual named accounts and payment accounts that could be embedded in third-party products. The role also covers selecting banking and payment partners and designing how client funds move. The companies have not announced a launch date.

For a payment firm, the intended sequence is to collect funds, convert them into a stablecoin for movement across borders, then convert and pay out in local currency. Stablecoin transfers can operate around the clock, while conventional cross-border payments often rely on banking hours and correspondent banks. Tether and SCRYPT say the investment is intended to help financial firms move liquidity between markets without keeping as much money in pre-funded accounts.

The amount Tether invested was not disclosed, and neither company specified the stake involved. Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino said banks, brokers and payment firms need ways to use stablecoins within their existing systems. SCRYPT founder and CEO Norman Wooding said the firm plans to expand into fiat accounts and payments with Tether’s backing.

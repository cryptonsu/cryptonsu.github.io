US wins authority over $1.2bn of Binance’s stolen crypto

The Justice Department won court authority over more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, within a wider effort that has frozen more than $2.7 billion.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 17, 2026 · 03:05 UTC2 min read#18efc1

The U.S. Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as authorities work with private-sector firms to stop the funds moving through crypto services. The department said on Nov. 12 that the combined effort had frozen more than $2.7 billion. Its announcement says the theft was carried out on or about Oct. 29 by North Korean military members the FBI calls TraderTraitor.

From the theft to the freeze

The FBI says TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance. The Associated Press reported the total at about $6.3 billion. That is the estimated theft, not the amount now under court authority.

After the theft, Justice Department divisions and law-enforcement partners worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the stolen funds. These providers are crypto businesses that can help block or freeze assets. The department says actors continued trying to launder the funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those services can move assets between networks, obscure their transaction trail or provide routes to trade them.

The agencies and their private-sector partners say they have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far. A freeze can stop assets from moving while investigators pursue them. Within that total, the department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. The distinction matters: the larger figure is the amount frozen through the combined effort; the smaller figure is the portion covered by court authorization.

Seizure does not settle ownership

A court-authorized seizure or restraint gives the government legal control to pursue assets. It does not, by itself, establish that the funds have been returned to Binance or to individual customers. The AP reported that the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds.

The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the stolen assets are ongoing. That leaves the final amount recovered, and who may ultimately receive it, unresolved. The $1.2 billion figure marks a legal step in the process; it is not a final accounting of the hack’s losses.

The Associated Press report also describes the $1.2 billion as part of the broader $2.7 billion freeze. The figures show how much authorities say has been blocked or brought under court authority so far, while recovery and forfeiture work continues.

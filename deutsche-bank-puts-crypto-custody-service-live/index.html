Deutsche Bank puts crypto custody service live

Deutsche Bank’s custody service is live for paying corporate and institutional clients in Europe, putting the bank in charge of selected crypto wallets and keys.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 11, 2026 · 23:23 UTC2 min read#63b6fa

Deutsche Bank’s digital asset custody service went live for paying corporate and institutional clients in Europe on Nov. 11, giving them a way to hold and transfer selected crypto assets with the bank managing the wallets and private keys. Reuters reported the launch. The bank’s September announcement said the service would initially support Bitcoin, Ether and selected stablecoins or e-money tokens, including USDC, EURC and EURAU.

What does the custody service do?

It puts Deutsche Bank in charge of the digital wallets and private keys that clients use to hold and transfer assets. A private key is the code that controls access to a digital asset; the bank’s role is like holding the keys to a vault on a client’s behalf. Clients can ask to safeguard assets and transfer them to third parties without building and running their own custody infrastructure, according to Deutsche Bank’s service announcement.

How does the bank protect the keys?

The system combines technical safeguards with controls over who can act. Deutsche Bank says it uses secure key generation and hardware protection. It separates responsibilities among staff and requires approval from more than one person for important actions. The setup also keeps assets in separate warm and cold storage environments, with backup and recovery arrangements. These controls are designed to reduce risks such as unauthorized access, lost keys and technical failure; they do not prevent the assets’ market prices from falling.

Who can use it, and what are its limits?

The service is aimed initially at eligible corporate and institutional clients in Europe, including companies, asset managers, hedge funds, custodians, brokers and sovereign institutions. Deutsche Bank’s Corporate Bank and Investment Bank serve the target groups. The service is not intended for retail clients, and onboarding depends on the bank’s due diligence and risk criteria.

Custody covers holding and transferring the supported assets. It does not insure clients against price moves, fraud or the failure of other market participants. Deutsche Bank says crypto-assets also lack protection comparable to the deposit-guarantee scheme for eligible bank deposits. The bank has said the supported asset range could expand subject to client demand, regulatory requirements and its own approval and risk processes. Tokenized financial instruments are on its roadmap, but the launch announcement describes that as a future plan, not part of the live custody service.

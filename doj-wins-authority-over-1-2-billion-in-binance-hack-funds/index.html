DOJ wins authority over $1.2 billion in Binance hack funds

The Justice Department won court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack funds, while the broader recovery remains ongoing.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 19, 2026 · 16:13 UTC2 min read#400476

A court authorization lets U.S. authorities pursue more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The Justice Department announced the authorization on Nov. 12, describing it as permission to seize or restrain the assets. That wording matters: the department did not say the money had been returned to Binance or its customers.

The FBI says North Korean military actors it calls TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about Oct. 29. After the theft, authorities and private companies worked with virtual asset service providers to freeze funds. Those providers can help stop assets from moving through services they operate. The Justice Department says the stolen funds are still being traced and that the hackers continue to launder them through crypto bridges, mixers and exchanges.

The $1.2 billion is part of a larger freeze

U.S. government and private-sector efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets, according to the Justice Department. The $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is part of that frozen total, not an additional recovery. The figures show how the response works in stages: first, freeze assets so they cannot readily move; then seek court authority to take or restrain specified funds.

The FBI has also urged virtual asset service providers to block transactions involving wallet addresses used to launder the stolen Binance assets. The department says the work to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit those assets is ongoing. A freeze can interrupt movement, but it does not by itself settle who legally owns the funds or how they will be distributed.

Forfeiture still determines who can claim recovered funds

Seizure or restraint is a legal step, not a payout. The Associated Press reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then considers who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s announcement does not say how much of the authorized amount has been seized rather than restrained, or when any funds might be returned.

For Binance users, the scale of the actions offers a measure of how much of the stolen crypto has been caught or legally targeted. But more than $6 billion was taken, and the authorities say laundering efforts continue. The court authorization advances recovery; it does not complete it.

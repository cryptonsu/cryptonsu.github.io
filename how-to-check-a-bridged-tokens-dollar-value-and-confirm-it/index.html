How to Check a Bridged Token's Dollar Value and Confirm It

A bridged token shows no dollar value in wallet when its contract lacks a price feed; compare addresses to confirm the asset and choose the next step.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 06:17 UTC3 min read#b10026

A bridged token with no dollar value in your wallet usually has a missing price feed, not a missing balance. Compare its contract address with the destination token recorded for the transfer in LI.FI Explorer, then check the issuer’s address list; a match confirms the route delivered the token it promised.

Your wallet can display a token balance without being able to price it. The balance comes from the token contract, while the dollar estimate comes from a separate price provider. A lifi bridge transfer can deliver a token whose price provider has not listed its contract.

Why the wallet may show $0

The most common cause is a missing price feed for the token’s contract on that chain. Wallets look up prices by contract address through providers such as CoinGecko. If a contract is unlisted or newly deployed, the wallet may show the token balance but no dollar value. That can happen even when the token itself is valid.

One ticker can refer to different contracts. On Arbitrum, native USDC and bridged USDC.e are separate tokens with separate addresses. A wallet may price one contract and have no price for the other. So a blank value alone cannot tell you whether the received token is the one you intended.

A less common possibility is that the route delivered a different token variant from the one you expected. A destination swap may have delivered a bridged asset such as USDC.e rather than native USDC, or another wrapped version. Its address will differ, and its price data may also be thin or absent. Treat the displayed ticker as a clue, not proof of identity.

Compare the contract addresses

The contract address is the useful identifier: compare the address for the token you received against the destination token recorded for your transfer. Find the transaction on the destination block explorer and copy the token contract address from the token or transaction details. Then look up the transfer by its hash in LI.FI Explorer, which records the source and destination tokens for that transfer.

Compare the full destination contract address in LI.FI Explorer with the address you copied. A match means the route delivered exactly the token it recorded, even if your wallet has no price for it. A different ticker does not overturn that result; the contract address is the comparison that settles whether the token matches.

For a second check, compare the address with the issuer’s official list for that chain. Circle’s USDC contract address list can confirm whether a token is native USDC there. CoinGecko token pages can help identify which contract and chain a listed price belongs to, but a missing listing is not evidence that a token is wrong.

If the addresses match, keep the token and contact your wallet provider if you need help with its missing dollar estimate. If they differ, the route delivered a different contract than the destination token recorded; verify the address before taking action. You can swap the received asset for the token you wanted on a DEX, but confirm the contract and trading pair first. Contact the relevant transfer provider if the transaction details do not match the transfer record. The address comparison tells you which issue to report.

Once the contract matches the intended destination token, save or note that address for future checks. It gives you a reliable way to identify the asset even when your wallet’s price feed is blank.

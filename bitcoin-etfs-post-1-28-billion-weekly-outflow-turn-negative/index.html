Bitcoin ETFs post $1.28 billion weekly outflow, turn negative

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost $1.28 billion in the week to Nov. 6, pushing 2026 flows $914 million negative and marking their heaviest withdrawals since June.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 10, 2026 · 06:06 UTC3 min read#0f86d5

U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs lost $1.28 billion in the week ended Nov. 6, pushing their 2026 net flows into negative territory, according to The Block’s report on the funds’ weekly flows. The withdrawals were the group’s largest weekly total since late June and erased the gains recorded earlier this year.

How do ETF flows connect to bitcoin?

An investor usually buys or sells ETF shares through a brokerage account. Those ordinary trades happen between market participants. To expand or shrink a fund’s share supply, authorized participants can create or redeem large blocks of shares, called baskets, with the trust in exchange for cash or bitcoin. The trust holds bitcoin, so creating or redeeming baskets can change how much bitcoin sits behind the shares. Think of the daily flow figure as a fund-level tally of money entering and leaving, rather than a count of every investor trade.

For example, BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) permits basket creation and redemption through authorized participants in exchange for cash or bitcoin, according to the fund’s prospectus information. When analysts report net outflows, they are describing the balance of those fund flows, not proving that every shareholder sold.

What happened during the week to Nov. 6?

U.S. spot bitcoin funds recorded outflows on four of the five trading days, The Block reported. Monday’s net outflow was $398.6 million, Tuesday’s was $115.6 million, and Thursday’s reached $430.2 million. Wednesday brought a $74.6 million inflow. Friday then saw $411.9 million leave the funds. The daily figures in Farside Investors’ flow table show the same pattern; together, the five sessions amounted to about $1.28 billion in net outflows.

BlackRock’s IBIT accounted for $403 million of Friday’s outflows, while Fidelity’s FBTC recorded a $42.8 million inflow that day. A positive result for one fund can sit alongside a negative total for the whole group because the reported figure adds flows across the funds.

How did the outflow week reverse the yearly total?

The funds entered the week with about $368 million in net inflows for 2026, after turning positive in late September, The Block reported. Monday’s withdrawals took the running total below zero. By Friday, the 12 U.S.-listed spot bitcoin funds had recorded about $914.1 million more in outflows than inflows for the year.

The weekly loss was the funds’ second consecutive negative week and ranked seventh among the largest weekly outflows since their launch in January 2024, according to The Block. The prior larger weekly decline was $1.79 billion in the week ended June 26. These flow totals track the balance of creations and redemptions; on their own, they do not identify why investors moved money or predict bitcoin’s next price move.

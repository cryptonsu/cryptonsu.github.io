US gets court authority over $1.2B in Binance hack crypto

The Justice Department won authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in crypto stolen from Binance, part of a wider freeze exceeding $2.7 billion.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 21, 2026 · 13:34 UTC2 min read#b2fdd1

The Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, after law enforcement and private companies froze more than $2.7 billion of the stolen assets. The Justice Department’s announcement says the theft happened on or about Oct. 29 and attributes it to members of North Korea’s military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor.

The distinction between a freeze and a seizure matters. A freeze blocks assets from moving through a service provider. The department says U.S. agencies worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the funds. Separately, it obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion within that larger frozen pool. The announcement does not say the full $1.2 billion has been transferred to government custody or returned to Binance.

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen

The total frozen so far combines government and private-sector efforts. The $1.2 billion covered by court authorization is part of that total, not an additional amount. The Associated Press report describes the theft as about $6.3 billion and says the FBI had previously blamed TraderTraitor.

To interrupt the movement of stolen crypto, investigators and service providers can identify addresses linked to the theft and block transactions involving those assets. The Justice Department says the actors continued trying to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. Those services can move assets between networks or obscure transaction trails, so the department says efforts to trace and freeze the funds are ongoing.

A court will determine claims to recovered funds

The court authorization allows the government to pursue seizure or restraint of the specified assets. It does not itself settle who ultimately owns them. In its report, AP says the U.S. government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then decides who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department’s announcement says tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts remain underway.

The reported theft exceeds $6 billion, while the frozen amount is more than $2.7 billion and the court-authorized portion is over $1.2 billion. Those figures describe different stages of the recovery process. For Binance users and other readers, the seizure announcement marks a major legal step, but it does not establish that all stolen funds have been recovered or that the assets covered by the court order have been returned.

EU proposal targets Russia-linked crypto networks and 60 banks

The European Commission’s 22nd Russia sanctions package would target crypto and payment networks, 60 banks and Russian sports participation if all EU states approve.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 27, 2026 · 10:52 UTC3 min read#e4569b

The European Commission proposed a new Russia sanctions package on 23 November that would extend transaction bans to about 60 Russian banks and target crypto and payment networks. The measures still need approval from all 27 EU member states, the Associated Press reported.

How would the financial measures work?

The proposal has two main financial steps: restrict transactions with named banks, then remove those banks from SWIFT, the messaging system banks use to communicate about international payments. Commission President Ursula von der Leyen said all 60 banks covered by the proposed extension would be removed from SWIFT.

For crypto, the Commission says it would target Russian crypto and payment networks, as well as third countries that help Russia evade EU sanctions. That makes the proposal broader than a measure aimed only at companies based in Russia. The Commission has not specified in its announcement which crypto platforms or third-country operators would be listed, or how each restriction would apply.

The Commission also said it had launched a Crypto Sanctions Task Force to support this work. Its statement did not set out the task force’s membership or operating powers.

What is the status of the 22nd package?

This is a proposal, not a set of sanctions already in force. The Commission presented the 22nd package on 23 November. EU member states must agree unanimously before the measures can take effect, and the details may change during that process, according to AP.

The Commission’s statement on the 22nd package describes the financial measures as part of a wider effort to limit Russia’s access to money and services connected to its war in Ukraine. The announcement does not provide a list of the banks or crypto networks in scope.

What else is in the proposal?

The package also proposes more than 70 additional ship listings and a ban on tankers insured in Russia. It would extend restrictions on European energy services to electricity services, and bar Europeans from serving on governing bodies of companies in Russia.

For sports, the Commission proposes barring Russian teams and athletes from competing under the Russian flag at events in the EU. It also proposes banning official Russian symbols at those events, restricting Russian sponsorship money to European sports and cultural associations, and limiting Russians’ roles in European sports federation governing bodies.

These measures remain part of the proposed package. Their practical effect will depend on the final text agreed by EU governments, including which financial platforms and institutions are named.

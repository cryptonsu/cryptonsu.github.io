How Does an Optimistic Relay in MEV-Boost Use Collateral?

An optimistic relay in MEV-Boost serves a builder’s bid before simulation, then checks the block; collateral covers invalid delivery and limits proposer risk.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 20, 2026 · 04:25 UTC4 min read#ba9d10

An optimistic relay in MEV-Boost forwards a builder’s bid to a proposer before simulating its block, relying on the builder’s collateral to compensate the proposer if the block is invalid.

A standard relay, such as the Flashbots Relay, simulates a block before offering its bid. An optimistic relay serves the bid first and checks the block afterwards, which can let a builder bid later in the slot.

For more on how this relay market fits together, read MEV-boost. Flashbots’ relay illustrates the standard sequence: validate the block, then make its bid available.

How an optimistic relay serves a bid before validation

An optimistic relay receives a builder’s bid and posted collateral, serves the block header to proposers without first simulating the block, then validates the block after serving it.

The builder constructs a block and submits its bid to the relay. The bid states the value offered to the proposer, while the collateral backs the builder’s promise that the block is valid. The relay checks that the bid falls within the builder’s collateral. If it exceeds that amount, the relay validates it normally before offering it.

For an eligible bid, the relay makes the header available immediately. A proposer using MEV-Boost can consider that bid alongside others, select one, and sign the blinded block. The relay then checks the full block. When it is valid, the relay delivers it and the proposer can publish the block for the slot.

Think of the relay as letting a bid pass through a fast lane with a deposit behind it. The deposit does not make an invalid block valid; it gives the proposer a route to compensation if the relay’s later check fails.

Why builders gain time and proposers carry bounded risk

Optimistic relaying gives builders more of the slot to construct and bid, while the proposer’s exposure is capped by the collateral backing the bid.

Say a builder finishes a block late in the slot and sends it to both an optimistic relay and a relay that simulates every block before serving its bid. The optimistic relay can forward its header at once. The simulating relay must spend time checking the block first, so its bid may arrive too late for the proposer to use it.

If the proposer selects the optimistic bid and the block passes the later check, the proposer receives the block and can publish it. If the check finds an invalid block, the proposer misses the slot: there is no valid block from that bid to publish in time. The builder’s collateral is used to compensate the proposer for that failure.

This arrangement shifts some risk to the proposer, but bounds that risk by the amount of collateral accepted for the bid. The relay does not treat every bid as safe regardless of size. It handles bids above the builder’s posted collateral through ordinary validation first.

An invalid submission also changes the builder’s future treatment. The relay demotes that builder to validated mode, so later bids must be checked before the relay serves them optimistically. That makes the speed advantage conditional: builders keep it by submitting valid blocks.

Related questions

Which MEV-Boost relays are optimistic?

The ultra sound relay introduced optimistic relaying in 2023. Its approach serves eligible bids before simulation, subject to collateral and the relay’s handling rules. Other relays may use a standard validation flow; check a relay’s own documentation to confirm its current behaviour.

What happens to a proposer if a relay delivers an invalid block?

The proposer misses the slot because the invalid block cannot be published as a valid proposal. Under optimistic handling, the builder’s collateral backs compensation for that failure, and the builder is demoted to validated mode. When choosing a bid, the proposer is therefore weighing its value against the collateral-backed risk and the relay’s validation process.

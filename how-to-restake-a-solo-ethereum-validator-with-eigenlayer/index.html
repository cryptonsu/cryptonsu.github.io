How to Restake a Solo Ethereum Validator With EigenLayer

Native restaking routes a solo Ethereum validator’s withdrawal credentials to an EigenPod, then proves its stake so it can secure selected AVSs.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 25, 2026 · 19:16 UTC3 min read#5cd474

When setting up a solo Ethereum validator, native restaking sends its withdrawal credentials to an EigenPod and proves the validator so the same 32 ETH can back chosen AVSs, bringing potential rewards and added slashing exposure.

A standard Ethereum validator starts with a 32 ETH deposit and secures the Ethereum Beacon Chain. Native restaking lets that stake also support additional services, called AVSs, through EigenLayer. Think of the stake as backing two sets of duties: Ethereum validation and the services you choose.

How do you prepare a validator for native restaking?

To prepare, create an EigenPod, set its address as the validator’s withdrawal credentials, then prove the active validator to the pod. The EigenPod is a smart contract that can receive withdrawals and track the validator’s restaked balance.

Before choosing credentials, plan the solo setup and run a 32 ETH validator with EigenLayer as part of that preparation. The eigen layer route depends on the pod address being set correctly from the start: Ethereum treats withdrawal credentials as a permanent destination once they are set.

After the validator is active, prove its credentials to the EigenPod. This proof lets EigenLayer verify that the validator points withdrawals to the pod and account for the stake toward restaking. The proof connects the Beacon Chain validator to its EigenPod; it does not replace the validator’s Ethereum duties.

Then choose how to participate in AVSs. You can run the software each service requires, or delegate AVS operations to an Operator. Delegation means an Operator handles service work for your restaked stake. In either case, opting in adds the service’s conditions, including possible slashing, to the risks attached to the stake.

Check existing withdrawal credentials before planning around a validator you already run. A validator with 0x01 credentials pointing to a wallet cannot simply redirect that address to an EigenPod. The credential destination cannot be changed after it is set, so native restaking may require exiting and activating a new validator with the pod address configured. A validator that still has 0x00 credentials may have a one-time credential update available; confirm its status and options before acting.

Keep the fee recipient separate in your setup. Withdrawal credentials control where validator stake and withdrawals go; the fee recipient is a different setting for execution rewards. Confirm that each address is set for its intended purpose before activating the validator.

For an illustrative case, say you are preparing one validator with 32 ETH and have created an EigenPod. You configure the pod as the withdrawal address, wait until the validator is active, then prove its credentials. After choosing an AVS, you either run its required software yourself or delegate its operations to an Operator. The same stake continues securing Ethereum while also backing that selected service, with potential additional rewards and the added risk of its slashing rules.

Related questions

What is an EigenPod?

An EigenPod is a smart contract used for native restaking. It receives a validator’s withdrawals and tracks its restaked balance so EigenLayer can account for the stake backing chosen AVSs.

Can an existing Ethereum validator be natively restaked?

It depends on its withdrawal credentials. A validator whose 0x01 address already points to a wallet cannot simply switch that address to an EigenPod; check the credential before planning a route into native restaking.

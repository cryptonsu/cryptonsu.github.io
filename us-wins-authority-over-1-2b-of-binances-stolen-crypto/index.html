US wins authority over $1.2B of Binance’s stolen crypto

The Justice Department has court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of Binance’s stolen crypto, while tracing and recovery efforts continue.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 15, 2026 · 11:57 UTC3 min read#6c8868

The U.S. Justice Department has court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, as investigators and private firms continue tracing the funds. The order applies to assets within a larger pool that has been frozen; it does not mean the government has completed forfeiture or returned the crypto.

In its November 12 announcement, the Justice Department said U.S. agencies and private-sector partners worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the stolen assets. Those providers can act on transfers involving accounts or services they control. The department did not specify which providers froze particular funds or describe the technical steps behind the court-authorized seizure or restraint.

More than $2.7 billion has been frozen

The reported totals describe different stages of the response. The combined government and private-sector effort has frozen more than $2.7 billion in stolen assets. Within that amount, the Justice Department has obtained court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion. The larger figure is the frozen pool; the smaller one is the amount covered by court authority.

That distinction matters because freezing assets can prevent further movement while legal proceedings continue. A seizure or restraint authorized by a court gives the government legal power over specified property. As the Associated Press reported, the government would typically file a forfeiture claim before a judge determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The Justice Department said tracing, freezing, seizure and forfeiture efforts remain ongoing.

The theft and laundering investigation continue

The Justice Department said members of North Korea’s military, whom the FBI calls TraderTraitor, stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or about October 29. The AP described the theft as about $6.3 billion. The FBI had previously attributed the theft to North Korea and warned that the stolen assets were being converted and laundered.

According to the Justice Department, TraderTraitor actors continue moving assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges. A bridge transfers tokens between blockchain networks. A mixer can pool or route transactions to make the movement of funds harder to trace. The department said the FBI, Secret Service and IRS criminal investigators are working together to recover the assets, and that the FBI has urged VASPs to block transactions linked to addresses used in the laundering.

The court authorization marks a legal step in controlling part of the stolen crypto. Whether those assets can ultimately be forfeited and returned remains unresolved in the government’s ongoing process.

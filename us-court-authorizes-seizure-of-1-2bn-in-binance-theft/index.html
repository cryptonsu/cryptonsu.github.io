US court authorizes seizure of $1.2bn in Binance theft

The court-authorized action covers part of more than $2.7 billion frozen after the October Binance theft, while investigators trace funds still moving through crypto services.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 14, 2026 · 18:14 UTC3 min read#71203b

US authorities have court authorization to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance, the Justice Department said Thursday. The order covers part of the funds frozen through cooperation between government agencies and private companies. The Justice Department announcement says tracing and recovery efforts are continuing.

The FBI attributes the theft to TraderTraitor, a group of North Korean military members. According to the Justice Department, the theft took place on or about October 29 and involved more than $6 billion in virtual assets. The Associated Press report says Binance reported the stolen assets last month.

How the freeze and seizure authority fit together

First, investigators and private-sector partners trace the stolen assets as they move between crypto addresses and services. The Justice Department says virtual asset service providers helped interdict the funds. In practical terms, an exchange or other provider can help stop assets passing through its service; that is like a checkpoint closing to a flagged transfer, not a recovery of every coin.

Those joint efforts have frozen more than $2.7 billion so far, according to the department. The newly authorized amount—more than $1.2 billion—is part of that total, not an additional sum. A freeze blocks or restricts movement. The court authorization allows the government to seize or restrain specified assets, but it does not by itself establish who ultimately owns them.

In a typical case, the government files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then considers competing claims to the recovered funds, AP reported. The Justice Department says efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the Binance assets remain underway. It also says the attackers are continuing to launder funds through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges.

More than $3 billion remains outside the reported freeze

The figures show the scale of the recovery effort, but they do not mean that all stolen crypto has been recovered. Comparing the reported theft of more than $6 billion with the more than $2.7 billion frozen leaves a gap of at least roughly $3.3 billion. That is an arithmetic comparison of the department’s figures, not a confirmed accounting of what remains in the attackers’ control: the announcement does not give a final balance or say how much can still be traced.

The FBI has asked virtual asset providers to block transactions with, or derived from, addresses it links to TraderTraitor. The government says investigators are working with those providers as the funds move across services. For Binance users and other crypto holders, the distinction matters: a court-authorized seizure is a legal step toward recovery, while the wider freeze total measures assets whose movement has been stopped so far.

Mesh and Visa link wallet balances to 175 million merchants

Mesh and Visa will let participating wallet apps route crypto, stablecoin and fiat balances to Visa payments, avoiding a separate card while rollout details remain pending.

By Crypto Readout EditorialOct 21, 2026 · 14:13 UTC3 min read#1e4bc5

Mesh and Visa announced a partnership on October 19 to let participating wallet apps turn users’ crypto, stablecoin and fiat balances into payments at Visa merchants, without requiring a separate card. Mesh says Visa’s network reaches more than 175 million merchant locations in over 200 countries and territories. In its partnership announcement, Mesh said it will handle the funding and conversion behind each payment while Visa provides the merchant network.

How does a wallet balance become a Visa payment?

A wallet or platform connects to Mesh, which orchestrates how a payment is funded and converted across supported balances. When a user pays in store or online, a Visa credential is provisioned behind the scenes. The user can tap at a standard contactless terminal or check out online; the merchant receives a standard payment and needs no new hardware or checkout changes, according to Mesh.

The flow resembles choosing a funding source at checkout, with Mesh handling the steps between the wallet balance and the payment. The announcement says users will not need to sell, transfer or preload funds before paying. It does not specify which assets will be supported by each participating platform or explain the conversion and settlement details for individual transactions.

Who will be able to use it, and when?

Wallets and platforms can offer the service through a Mesh integration instead of building and operating their own card program. Mesh says it will act as program manager, handling user onboarding and know-your-customer checks. The Block’s report on the announcement likewise notes that initial availability, supported devices and participating platforms have not yet been announced.

Mesh named Kalshi, Tools for Humanity, Félix Pago, MAJORITY and KAST as enterprise partners, but did not say those companies will be among the first to offer Visa payments through this arrangement. The rollout details are still pending, so the announced merchant reach describes Visa’s network, not confirmed live access for Mesh users.

What changes for merchants?

The payment arrives through Visa’s existing acceptance system, rather than as a direct crypto transfer to the merchant. That means merchants do not need to accept crypto themselves or alter checkout, according to Mesh. The company reported that more than 200 enterprises already use its network; the announcement did not say how many wallet platforms will initially connect to the Visa service.

For users, the proposed change is where the payment starts: a balance held in a participating wallet app, rather than funds first moved to a bank account or a separate card. Whether that becomes a practical everyday option will depend on which platforms join and when they launch.

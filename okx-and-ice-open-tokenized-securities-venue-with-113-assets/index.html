OKX and ICE open tokenized securities venue with 113 assets

OKX and ICE opened a tokenized securities venue with 113 assets confirmed for phased listing, while 67 companies objected to share tokens, testing issuer control.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 24, 2026 · 12:59 UTC3 min read#1bab20

A liquidity pool sets a token’s trading price from the ratio of assets in the pool; OKX and Intercontinental Exchange (ICE) are using that model to bring tokenized U.S. securities to OKXICE, their joint venture. The venue opened on Nov. 23 with six stocks available and 113 assets confirmed for phased listing, according to CoinDesk’s report on the launch.

The process starts with an existing share. A token represents an entitlement backed one-to-one by an underlying share, according to OKXICE’s description of the venue. A participant must pass identity checks and be approved for trading. Then the participant can trade through permissioned automated market maker (AMM) pools: smart contracts govern trades between assets held in the pool, rather than matching buy and sell orders in an order book. The pools use Uniswap v4 on OKX’s X Layer network.

At launch, the six available stocks were Coinbase, Circle, Robinhood, Strategy, BitGo and Securitize. The remaining assets are to be added on a rolling basis. OKXICE said the 113 confirmed assets include stocks, exchange-traded funds and other exchange-traded products; it did not provide CoinDesk with a full list. Fidelity Investments, eToro, Strive and Vida Global were among the additional participants named for the venue. Direxion plans to bring 59 ETFs.

The launch follows objections from companies whose shares OKXICE had proposed to tokenize. CoinDesk counted objections from 56 of the 62 companies on the initial list, including Apple, Microsoft, Nvidia and JPMorgan Chase. The total reached 67 after objections from companies beyond that original group. An objection prevents a third party’s tokenized version of that company’s stock from being traded on the venue.

That right comes from the Securities and Exchange Commission’s temporary, conditional exemption for tokenized securities venues. The SEC’s order in the Federal Register requires a venue to notify an issuer when an unaffiliated third party tokenizes its stock. Trading cannot begin until at least 30 calendar days after the issuer receives notice. If the issuer objects within that period, the venue cannot list the token.

The exemption also sets conditions for the venue and the tokens. It permits qualifying venues to use permissioned AMM pools, where smart contracts set prices based on the relative quantities of assets in the pool. The tokens must provide the same rights and privileges as the corresponding shares, including dividend and voting rights. OKXICE says its tokens represent existing shares rather than new securities offerings.

The result is a launch with a broad confirmed pipeline but a narrow first set of stocks. OKXICE has opened a blockchain-based route for trading securities, while the objection process gives issuers a defined chance to keep unaffiliated token versions of their shares off that venue.

Pay a Polygon Invoice by Matching the Network, Token and Address

Pay a Polygon invoice by matching its network, token contract, amount and recipient, then send the stablecoin from a funded wallet and check confirmation.

By Crypto Readout EditorialSep 29, 20262 min read#aa657d

To pay a Polygon invoice with stablecoins, select the specified Polygon network in your wallet, then send the requested token and amount to the invoice’s address. The transfer is an on-chain token transaction: your wallet signs it, the network records it, and the recipient can verify it. The invoice’s currency symbol alone is not enough to identify the right asset.

What details should a Polygon invoice include?

A usable invoice identifies the network, token, amount, recipient address and any payment deadline or reference. Polygon can refer to more than one network, so check which one the merchant accepts rather than assuming that any wallet labelled “Polygon” will do. On Polygon PoS, for example, the network’s chain ID is 137.

The token also needs to match. Different tokens can share a ticker or name while using different contracts, and a bridged token may not be interchangeable with the version the merchant accepts. For USDC, Polygon PoS has native USDC and a bridged version known as USDC.e. Check the merchant’s instructions for the contract or an explicit token label. The Polygon Bridge moves assets between networks; its token support and contract details matter when funds need to be bridged before payment.

How do you send the stablecoin from a wallet?

First, switch the wallet to the invoice’s exact network and check that it displays the expected stablecoin balance there. If the funds are on another chain, move them using a supported bridge or exchange route before paying; a balance on Ethereum, for instance, does not count as a Polygon balance.

Next, start a token transfer, paste the recipient address and enter the invoice amount. Review the wallet’s confirmation screen before signing. The wallet submits a transaction to the network, which processes the token transfer from your address to the recipient’s. It is similar to writing a cheque: the account, payee and amount all have to match, but here the network also has to match.

  • Confirm the network shown in both the invoice and wallet.
  • Match the token by its contract or the merchant’s stated version, not just its ticker.
  • Compare the full recipient address and payment amount with the invoice.
  • Keep enough POL on Polygon PoS to pay the network fee.

Stablecoins cover the invoice value, but they do not automatically cover transaction fees. On Polygon PoS, ordinary wallet transactions require POL for gas. If you have the stablecoin but no POL, the wallet may be unable to submit the payment.

How can you check that the invoice is paid?

After signing, the wallet shows a transaction hash or status. Use it to check that the transaction succeeded on the same network and that the token transfer went to the invoice address for the required amount. A submitted transaction is not yet proof of completion; wait for it to be confirmed and follow the merchant’s stated payment rules.

If the details are wrong, pause before signing. Token transfers usually cannot be reversed by the sender, and sending a different token or using the wrong network may leave the merchant unable to credit the payment. The reliable sequence is simple: match the network, identify the token, verify the address and amount, fund the gas fee, then check the confirmed transfer.

NYSE selects Avalanche ledger for tokenized stock platform

NYSE’s planned digital trading venue will use Avalanche technology for its clearing ledger, linking tokenized shares across networks if regulators approve.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 10, 2026 · 12:37 UTC3 min read#5c4da7

The New York Stock Exchange’s planned digital trading venue will use Avalanche technology for its clearing ledger, giving the platform a blockchain layer to record and settle tokenized stock trades. Ava Labs announced the selection on Nov. 9, saying the NYSE-affiliated alternative trading system, or ATS, is designed for round-the-clock trading and settlement, subject to regulatory approval.

The process has separate steps. The venue matches orders; its clearing firm handles the post-trade process; and a ledger records and settles the trades. The NYSE plans to pair its Pillar trading engine with blockchain-based clearing, settlement and custody, according to CoinDesk’s report on the platform. Ava Labs says Avalanche will provide the core ledger technology to the platform’s clearing firm.

What role will Avalanche play?

Avalanche will supply ledger technology; the announcement does not say the platform will settle on Avalanche’s public network. Ava Labs chief business officer John Nahas told CoinDesk that the NYSE will operate a custom blockchain built using Avalanche technology and tailored to the platform’s regulatory needs.

The distinction matters because the ledger is the recordkeeping and settlement layer, not the order-matching engine. In practical terms, the venue’s trading system pairs buyers and sellers, while the ledger records the trade and supports its settlement. The chain is like a shared transaction book: the clearing firm uses it to record and settle trades under rules that control who can participate.

Will all the tokenized stocks be issued on Avalanche?

No. NYSE says the platform is designed to support equities listed on a range of blockchains. Those assets would connect to the custom Avalanche-based chain used to record and settle trades, according to the Ava Labs announcement carried by PR Newswire.

That design separates where a tokenized equity is issued from the ledger used for settlement. NYSE digital issuance head Dennis Lee said the settlement ledger needs to be fast and reliable, keep costs predictable and let the exchange control participation. The announcement gives no launch date or name for the clearing firm.

When could the platform begin trading?

The platform is planned, not yet approved or live. Ava Labs said the NYSE-affiliated ATS is designed to support 24/7 trading, clearing and settlement for subscribers, subject to regulatory approvals. The announcement did not specify which regulators must approve it or when a decision is expected.

If approved, the venue would combine a conventional matching engine with blockchain-based post-trade infrastructure. That setup would let it support tokenized equities from multiple networks while using one designated ledger for clearing and settlement. For now, Avalanche’s role is a technology selection for a planned market; the announcement confirms no completed trades, operating venue or approval.

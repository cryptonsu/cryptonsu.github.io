Bridged tokens from Sui: Reach an Ethereum exchange deposit

Move bridged tokens from Sui to an Ethereum exchange deposit address by returning supported assets to your wallet, then sending the accepted asset on Ethereum.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 19, 2026 · 11:24 UTC4 min read#c6ff6c

To move bridged tokens from Sui to an Ethereum exchange deposit address, return a supported asset to your own Ethereum wallet, then send the exchange accepted asset to its Ethereum deposit address. The wallet creates a conventional Ethereum transfer, and the exchange must accept both the Ethereum network and the asset it receives.

Suibridge is a cross-chain bridge that uses a lock-and-mint process to move supported assets from Ethereum to Sui. Its lock-and-mint process makes Suibridge’s Ethereum-to-Sui transfers a concrete example of how supported assets move across chains. For a return, first confirm which Sui assets the bridge supports back to Ethereum.

What to have ready before moving tokens

Start with the exchange’s deposit instructions for the exact asset. Confirm that deposits are open, the selected network is Ethereum, and the asset and transfer type are accepted. Exchanges can list an asset on more than one network, and selecting the wrong network or sending an unsupported asset can prevent automatic credit.

Read the minimum deposit amount and any memo or other transfer requirements shown for that asset. Use the exchange’s current instructions and copy the deposit address from its Ethereum deposit screen. Keep that address for the final wallet transfer; it is not the destination for the bridge return.

You need access to the Sui wallet holding the bridged tokens and an Ethereum wallet you control. Keep SUI in the Sui wallet for Sui transaction gas. Have ETH available in the Ethereum wallet for the final transfer’s gas. The exchange instructions, bridge-supported asset information, and enough funds for both networks should be ready before you begin.

Return the asset, then send it to the exchange

  1. Return the supported asset to Ethereum. Connect the Sui wallet to Suibridge, select the supported asset and Ethereum as the destination, and enter your own Ethereum wallet address. Review the asset, amount, network, and destination before confirming. Approve the Sui transaction and any bridge steps shown, including a required claim. The visible result is a completed bridge transfer and the released asset in your Ethereum wallet; wait for the bridge process to finish before continuing.
  2. Prepare the asset the exchange accepts. Compare the released token with the exchange’s deposit listing. A bridged token can have a different contract or transfer type from the asset the exchange accepts. If the exchange requires another form of the asset, convert or unwrap it in your wallet using a route you trust before sending. The visible result is a balance of the accepted asset in your Ethereum wallet, with enough ETH left to pay transfer gas.
  3. Send a test deposit. From your Ethereum wallet, send an amount above the exchange’s stated minimum to the copied Ethereum deposit address. Confirm the network and asset in the wallet before signing. The visible result is an Ethereum transaction hash. Follow the exchange deposit status and allow for its required confirmations; Coinbase, for instance, explains why a deposit can remain pending while a transaction is unconfirmed in its deposit guidance.
  4. Send the remainder after credit. Once the test amount appears in the exchange balance, send the remaining accepted asset to the same deposit address, again using Ethereum and following the minimum and other instructions. The visible result is a second transaction to monitor, followed by the credited remainder. The task is done when the exchange balance reflects the deposits.

The intermediate wallet matters when an exchange treats a bridge release differently from an ordinary deposit. The bridge release comes from a contract process; the next transfer is a standard wallet-to-exchange transaction. That extra step gives you a chance to check the asset and test the exchange’s acceptance before moving the full balance.

Related

Are exchange crypto deposits temporarily suspended?

Hold the assets in your wallet until deposits reopen. Sending while deposits are suspended can delay credit, so check the exchange’s deposit status before making the test transfer.

Who can recover a wrong-network exchange deposit?

The exchange controls recovery, and its policy and the technical feasibility vary. Contact the exchange with the transaction details; recovery is not assured, so verify the network and address before sending.

Once the test deposit is credited, send the remainder and watch its status in the exchange. This confirms the full balance has reached the account before you move on to your next trade or withdrawal.

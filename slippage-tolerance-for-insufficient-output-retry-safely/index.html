Slippage tolerance for insufficient output: retry safely

Slippage tolerance for an insufficient output amount error: refresh the quote, then raise the minimum-output allowance only enough for modest price movement.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 20, 2026 · 16:29 UTC4 min read#f0711a

Slippage tolerance sets how much a token swap’s output may fall below its quote before the transaction is rejected. After an insufficient output amount error, refresh the quote and raise tolerance only enough to cover modest expected movement; the router reverts because the available output fell below the minimum calculated from the quote and tolerance.

On Fraxswap, the Fraxswap Router checks that minimum against the output available when the swap executes. The same mechanism explains Fraxswap’s insufficient-output error fix: understanding the rejection helps you decide whether a refreshed quote or a different retry is needed.

Start with the failed swap and its refreshed quote. Make sure your wallet is on the intended network and has enough gas for a retry. Then confirm the token pair and route, and compare the new expected output with the earlier quote. A changed quote may reflect movement in the market or a different route; either way, use the current quote to set your minimum.

How should I adjust slippage tolerance after the error?

On Fraxswap, set the tolerance so the minimum output allows modest expected movement, then review that minimum before confirming. In a Uniswap-style AMM, the router calculates the minimum from the quoted output and the permitted percentage difference. A transaction reverts if execution would return less than that threshold.

Say the quote is 100 tokens. At 1% tolerance, the minimum output is 99 tokens; at 2%, it is 98. Raising tolerance lowers the minimum, giving the swap more room to execute if the price moves before confirmation. It does not improve the quoted rate. Instead, you accept a wider gap between the quote and the least output you are willing to receive.

Compare the minimum output itself, not just the percentage setting. A small percentage change can matter when the quoted output is large, and a low minimum may allow a fill you would regret. Review the expected output, minimum output, pair, route and transaction details together before confirming. The quote may also stop being usable, so refresh it if there has been a delay.

Consider two swaps with the same token pair, pool conditions and quote freshness. The only difference is trade size. A smaller trade may need only a modest tolerance to accommodate ordinary movement. A larger trade can put more pressure on the available liquidity, so a wider tolerance might be needed to clear the minimum. If that makes the minimum unacceptably low, reduce the trade size and quote again rather than accepting a poor rate.

Fraxswap pair contracts handle swaps, while the router applies the minimum-output check for the transaction. Think of that check like a floor: execution can proceed above it, but an output below it triggers a revert. The tolerance changes where the floor sits; it does not control what the pool will quote or guarantee what price the market offers.

If the refreshed expected output is close to the earlier quote and the minimum remains acceptable, a small tolerance increase may be a reasonable retry. If the quote has moved sharply, the route looks wrong, or the required allowance seems too high, pause. Wait for steadier conditions or try a smaller amount, then refresh the quote and reassess.

Related

What does slippage tolerance mean on a DEX?

On a DEX, slippage tolerance is the permitted difference between the quoted output and the minimum output a swap can return. A higher setting lowers that minimum and gives execution more room as conditions change.

How do I reduce slippage on a token swap?

To reduce slippage on a token swap, try a smaller trade or wait for steadier conditions, then refresh the quote and inspect the route. If volatility, pool liquidity, trade size or quote freshness changes, review the tolerance again: calmer conditions may let you lower it, while more movement may require a slightly wider allowance or a decision to wait.

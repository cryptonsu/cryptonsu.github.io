US wins court authority over $1.2bn in Binance theft

The Justice Department has court authority over more than $1.2 billion of Binance hack funds, while broader freezes exceed $2.7 billion as investigators trace remaining assets.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 17, 2026 · 14:24 UTC3 min read#e248a9

The U.S. Justice Department says it has obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in cryptocurrency stolen from Binance. The order gives investigators a legal route to act on identified assets while they trace the theft. In its Nov. 12 announcement, the department described the authority and the wider effort to freeze funds in its account of actions against North Korean cryptocurrency theft.

The $1.2 billion is part of a larger freeze

The distinction between a freeze and a seizure matters. A freeze blocks assets from moving, while seizure or restraint is a court-authorized step toward taking control of them. DOJ said the U.S. government and private-sector partners had frozen more than $2.7 billion connected to the Binance theft. More than $1.2 billion of that total has court authorization for seizure or restraint. The figures describe overlapping stages of the recovery effort, not separate pools of money.

That means the court authority does not, by itself, establish that the assets have been returned to Binance or its customers. The Associated Press reported that the government typically files a forfeiture claim with a judge, who then determines who has a legitimate claim to recovered funds. The AP report on the FBI announcement also described the $1.2 billion as part of the larger frozen total.

Investigators are tracing transfers across crypto services

DOJ said North Korean military members known to the FBI as TraderTraitor stole more than $6 billion in virtual assets from Binance on or around Oct. 29. The AP put the theft at about $6.3 billion and said the FBI had previously attributed it to the group. The difference between the stolen amount and the frozen amount shows the scale of assets investigators are still pursuing.

After the theft, investigators worked with virtual asset service providers, or VASPs, to interdict the funds. These firms can help block transfers involving assets they can identify. DOJ said the actors continue to launder the stolen assets through cryptocurrency bridges, mixers and exchanges, which move or obscure funds across services. The FBI has urged VASPs to block transactions involving addresses linked to the laundering.

Recovery and forfeiture remain in progress

The court-authorized amount is a concrete step in recovery, but the process is ongoing. DOJ said efforts to trace, freeze, seize and forfeit the assets continue. A court will determine legitimate claims to funds that enter forfeiture; the announcement did not say that the $1.2 billion had been distributed or returned. For Binance users, the confirmed development is that authorities and private firms have frozen more than $2.7 billion, with court authority covering over $1.2 billion of it.

Berachain bridge for beginners: what sets transfer speed

Is the Berachain bridge faster than Stargate? Not always: source-chain confirmations, route design and Stargate Taxi or Bus mode determine when assets arrive.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 23, 2026 · 13:31 UTC4 min read#9d38f7

Berachain bridge users choosing a faster route should compare the transfer mode and route, because neither brand is always faster. Both routes can wait for the source transaction to confirm and for the cross-chain message to be verified and executed. Stargate’s official transfer documentation explains Taxi and Bus modes, while LayerZero’s DVN documentation describes how message verification depends on configured source-chain confirmations.

A Berachain asset bridge moves crypto between Berachain and another blockchain so the assets can be used in Berachain’s DeFi ecosystem. For a way to move assets to or from Berachain, berachainbridge.app provides a Berachain bridge.

What sets transfer time on the Berachain bridge versus Stargate?

A cross-chain transfer starts with a transaction on the source chain. The source chain records and confirms it; the route’s messaging or relayer system processes the transfer; then a transaction executes on Berachain and the tokens arrive there. Think of the route as a delivery: sending the parcel is only the first part; the source chain must confirm it before the next leg can proceed.

On message-based routes, source-chain confirmations are often the largest waiting step. LayerZero messages wait for the number of block confirmations configured for that source-chain pathway. Afterward, the configured Decentralized Verifier Networks (DVNs) verify the message, and destination execution releases the transfer. A route with more confirmations to wait for can take longer before that final step begins.

Stargate V2 adds another timing choice. Taxi sends a one-to-one transfer once source-chain finality is reached. Bus batches transfers together to share messaging costs, so the transfer waits for the bus to depart—when it fills or reaches its waiting limit. Bus can be cheaper, but its batching adds a wait that Taxi avoids. That means “Stargate speed” depends on whether the quote offers and selects Taxi or Bus.

Intent-based routes use a different path. With Across Protocol, a relayer can fill a transfer on the destination chain using its own funds, then settle with the protocol later. This can deliver tokens in seconds on supported routes, because the destination fill need not wait for the same message-and-execution sequence. Actual availability and timing depend on the specific route and quote.

So the visible difference is the transfer mode or mechanism: Taxi sends directly after finality, Bus waits to batch, and a relayer route fills the destination intent. Treating a brand name as a timing promise confuses those moving parts. Before choosing, compare the estimated arrival time shown in the quote for your source chain, destination, asset and mode. For a bridge from Ethereum to Berachain, use the quote for that particular route; another chain pair may have a different confirmation wait.

Related questions

How long does bridging to Berachain take?

Bridging to Berachain takes as long as the selected route needs for source confirmations, message verification or relayer fill, and destination execution. The estimate in the route quote is the useful starting point for your particular transfer; a Bus option can add batching time, while Taxi skips that wait.

Why is my bridge transfer taking so long?

A bridge transfer may be waiting for source-chain confirmations, DVN verification, a Stargate Bus departure, or destination execution. Check the transaction status and route mode: a submitted Bus transfer can still be waiting for its batch, while a message-based route may still be in its confirmation or verification stage.

Three more bridge questions

Does a faster bridge cost more?

Often, but compare the actual quote. Stargate’s Taxi is the faster one-to-one option and Bus shares messaging costs through batching. Other route fees depend on the transfer and current conditions, so the speed and amount received in the quote help show the trade-off.

Is a faster bridge less secure?

Speed alone does not tell you how secure a route is. Message-based routes rely on their configured confirmation threshold and verifiers; intent routes rely on relayers to fill transfers and protocol settlement afterward. Compare the route’s security design as well as its estimate.

What does inflight mean on a bridge explorer?

“Inflight” generally means the transfer has started but has not reached its completed destination state. The source transaction may be confirmed while verification, batching, relayer fill or destination execution remains pending. Check the route status for the current stage, then choose the route whose quoted time fits your transfer.

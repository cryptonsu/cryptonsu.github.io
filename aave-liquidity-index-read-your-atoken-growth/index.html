Aave Liquidity Index: Read Your aToken Growth

The Aave liquidity index tracks supplier interest as a shared multiplier; compare today’s index with your deposit index to calculate aToken growth.

By Crypto Readout EditorialNov 28, 2026 · 02:23 UTC4 min read#97a46a

1 RAY, or 1e27, is the starting value of each Aave liquidity index and the baseline for measuring supplier growth.

The liquidity index is a per-reserve multiplier: your aToken balance is your scaled balance multiplied by the reserve’s current index. Aave stores the index in RAY units, a high-precision format used for on-chain calculations.

That index is also separate from Aave staking, which uses staking mechanisms to support protocol risk management and earn rewards. For the related question of Aave staking’s yield and slash risk, read our analysis before comparing staking rewards with supplier interest.

The liquidity index updates a shared supplier multiplier

Aave’s liquidity index tracks the supply interest earned in a reserve without rewriting every supplier’s balance. On deposit, the protocol divides the supplied amount by the current index and records the result as the supplier’s scaled balance.

Say you deposit 1,000 USDC when the index is 1.05. Your scaled balance is about 952.38. That figure stays as the accounting base while the reserve index moves.

When the reserve is updated, Aave uses the supply rate and the time since the previous update to increase the index. In simplified terms, the new index is the previous index multiplied by 1 + supply rate × elapsed time. The rate is annualized in the protocol’s calculations, so the elapsed interval is normalized to the year.

The supply rate depends on reserve economics: supply rate = borrow rate × utilization × (1 − reserve factor). Utilization measures how much of the supplied liquidity is borrowed. The reserve factor is the share of interest set aside for the protocol rather than suppliers.

If the index later reaches 1.071, your balance reads about 1,020 USDC: 952.38 × 1.071. The extra 20 USDC is reflected in the balance even though Aave did not need to update your individual scaled balance each time the reserve changed.

The index ratio measures growth across changing rates

To calculate your growth, divide today’s liquidity index by the index at your deposit block. That ratio gives your exact index-based growth factor across the period, even if the supply rate and APY changed along the way.

For the deposit above, 1.071 ÷ 1.05 is about 1.02, or 2% growth. Multiply your original amount by that factor to estimate the current balance: 1,000 × 1.02 = 1,020 USDC. This comparison captures the path the index took without requiring you to reconstruct each past APY.

Update timing matters. Aave updates reserve state during relevant reserve actions, such as a supply or borrow. Between those updates, getReserveNormalizedIncome projects the index forward to the current timestamp using the stored rate and elapsed time. The projected value lets the protocol calculate a current aToken balance without waiting for another reserve action.

The liquidity index accrues linearly over each interval between updates. The variable borrow index uses compounding instead. Mixing them up can lead to the wrong estimate: a supplier’s aToken growth follows the liquidity index, while a variable-rate borrower’s debt follows the variable borrow index. Both respond to reserve activity, but they track different balances and use different index calculations.

In practice, the scaled balance is the stable quantity to pair with an index reading. Your wallet’s displayed aToken balance can rise as the normalized income is projected, even when you have made no new transaction. An ERC-4626 vault built on top of Aave may present its own share accounting as well, so distinguish vault shares from the underlying aToken balance when checking a position.

Related

What is the variable borrow index in Aave?

The variable borrow index tracks how variable-rate debt grows for borrowers. It is distinct from the liquidity index, which is used to calculate supplier aToken balances.

Why does my aToken balance rise without transactions?

Your scaled balance can stay fixed while the reserve’s normalized liquidity index grows. The displayed balance is that scaled amount multiplied by the current index.

To check your own earnings, note the liquidity index at your deposit block, retrieve the current normalized income for the same reserve, and divide the current reading by the earlier one. That gives you the growth factor to apply to your deposited amount.
